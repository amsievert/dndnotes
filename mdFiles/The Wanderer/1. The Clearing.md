A tired head poked through the flaps of the tent. Wise, aged eyes scanned the small clearing that the tent sat near the edge of. Rays of sunlight pierced through a thin haze that hung in the air. The ground sparkled with dew illuminated by the morning sun. The man from the tent slowly and methodically folded back the fabric around the tent's entrance. He rose with a grunt and wandered towards the center of the clearing where last night's campfire lay black and smoldering. As he walked, he spread his arms wide, stretching aching joints in preparation for the day. The many days of travel had taken a toll on his body. Despite the discomfort, he enjoyed the wilds more than anything. In reality, he found himself taking more frequent stops while traveling, not to rest, but instead to enjoy the scenery.

The man grabbed a stick that lay against the small ring of stones and poked at the smoldering remains. They hissed as dew found fresh coals, throwing small puffs of steam into the air. Poking at the fire reminded the old man's body of something. FOOD! His stomach grumbled and the man's attention immediately shifted away from the fire. He looked toward the other side of the clearing where his pack sat against a large boulder. Rations were beginning to become a concern, but the situation wasn't dire quite yet. He strode over to the boulder and unfastened the pack, searching inside. Pushing past books and scrolls, his hand found the small pack of dried meat wrapped in fabric near the bottom of the pack. His steady hands untied the twine on the package and unfolded the rough fabric. Inside were multiple slabs of dried venison. He grabbed one and held it between his teeth as he repackaged the rest and stowed them away in the pack once again.

The old man leaned against the boulder, gathering his senses and mentally preparing for the day. He planned to continue travelling west, away from the rising sun. Many considered the Western Reaches to be completely uninhabited, but few knew the depth of its history. These lands had once been home to a thriving civilization of people that worshipped the primordial gods. Even the greatest scholars knew incredibly little about the civilization's way of life, including their worship, which was based entirely on inference and conjecture. Despite their mystery, this ancient people had left their mark on the land. All across the continent, all the way out to the east, they had erected stone monoliths, tall as mountains, with divine precision. Fittingly, they were known today as the First Builders.

As the man's mind wandered, he suddenly felt movement against his back. He snapped out of his daydream and spun quickly on his heel. A small form crawled across the face of the boulder where he had been leaning. The man grabbed his staff that was leaning nearby and pointed it towards the moving stone. The form screeched in a high-pitched voice and froze.
"Sorry, didn't mean to alarm you" the small voice squeaked.
The old man paused for a moment, studying the creature in front of him. The creature was about the size of his hand, with four legs that protruded from a round body. The whole creature looked to be made of rough stone, yet it was very much alive. Two tiny glowing specks on the body of the creature looked toward the old man. He presumed these to be eyes of some sort. *A forest sprite of some sort*, the man concluded.
His expression softened and he planted his staff into the ground. "No little one, it is I who must apologize to you. I am merely a visitor here in your home."
"Oh, it's okay. Honestly, I wasn't expecting to see any humans out here. It's not very often anymore that we see your kind in these woods," the sprite said as its body calmed down.
The man raised an eyebrow. "Not anymore? So you've seen humans here in the past?" he inquired.
"Well, I've never seen any personally, but I've heard stories from others much older than me," the sprite conceded.
"And what do these elders' stories say?" the man probed.
"Apparently, humans like you used to live throughout these woods, lots of them. I've heard they got along very well with our ancestors, and that they were very respectful to the forest," the voice continued, "I've also heard that humans these days aren't as nice..." the voice trailed off. The forest sprite shifted nervously.
The man smiled politely, "I promise I'm not here to disturb the forest at all." He raised a hand, palm open to the creature. "I'm just an old man looking for what happened to these ancient humans out here."
The creature paused for a moment, studying the man and weighing his words. "Very well. I'll let the other sprites know that you're passing through so no one else gets surprised," it finally responded.
"That's very kind of you." The man bowed slightly. "Say, what do they call you? So that I may pass your good tidings onto others of your ilk that I meet."
"They called me The One Who Scurries," the sprite responded triumphantly. "And what do they call you human?"
The man furrowed his brow and stroked his long beard, thinking. "I suppose I don't really have a name that I care to be called anymore," he said pensively.
The creature's body cocked to the side inquisitively. "You are a strange one indeed," the sprite admitted as it's glowing eyes looked the man up and down. "I shall call you Gray," it concluded.
The man spread his arms slightly and look down at his gray cloak. "Yes, that will do great," he said before asking, "One more question. Do you know where those old humans used to live?"
One Who Scurries thought for a moment. "I'm not sure exactly, but the first place to look would be that way." The creature raised a leg to point West.
Gray nodded. "Thank you very much, One Who Scurries. I hope we meet again someday," the man smiled and bowed deeply this time.
"Likewise." The forest sprite turn and scurried along the face of the rock until it left Gray's view.
The man waited for a moment and then hoisted his pack onto a shoulder. He turned and walked back towards his camp.

Gray packed up the rest of his camp, kicking dirt over the fire and packing his tent before strapping it to the bottom of his pack. He looked around the clearing once more. Using his staff as a walking stick, he left the clearing heading west.
